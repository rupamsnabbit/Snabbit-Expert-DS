Core component

Timer

Time as a shape. A pill whose stroke drains as time runs out, digits inside, one word above saying what the time is for. It runs the three moments that decide whether she gets paid.

Urgency

Tones

Colour is a function of time remaining, never set by hand — so urgency means the same thing on every screen. Every tone change is also a sound and, past 60 s, a haptic. The stroke length, digits and sound change together; colour is never alone.

Forms

Modes & sizes

Countdown drains, elapsed fills, deadline has no stroke at all — a time you cannot run out of should not look like one you can. L for full-screen moments, M for cards and headers.

Try it

Live: the job alert

A 30-second window. Watch the tone change at 15 s and 6 s, the pulse under 10 s, and what happens at zero. The Timer sits directly above the accept Button — not around it; a ring around a 56 dp button is too thin to read at arm's length.

Why this form

The existing Figma Timer already had the right idea — ring, digits, urgency colour. What changes: the colour is computed from time remaining instead of chosen per instance, digits drop the leading zero (4:23 not 04:23), the label goes to sentence case, and the "Login by 6:45 am" variant becomes a separate deadline mode with no draining stroke.

Placement diagram, not the final screen — the full job-alert pattern arrives once Amount, Timer and the pictograms are all specified.

Reconciliation

What changes in Figma

  1. Merge the three Timer frames. Properties: Mode (Countdown / Elapsed / Deadline), Size (L / M), Tone (Calm / Attention / Urgent / Expired — documentation only; computed in code).
  2. Digits m:ss / mm:ss / h:mm:ss, no leading zero on the first group.
  3. Label to sentence case, BodyMd (L) / Caption (M).
  4. Deadline: borderBrand outline, no progress stroke, digits textBrand.
  5. Bind stroke and digits to status tokens, track to borderDefault.

Result: ~18 variants.
