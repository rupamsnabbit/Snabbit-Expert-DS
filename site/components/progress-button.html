ProgressButton

A Button whose fill moves on its own — the clock or the server moves it, not her finger. countdown shows how much of a window has been used (Accept job · 0:42) where there is no Timer on the screen; progress shows how far the work she started has got (Checking in · 2 of 3). The band always grows from the left, the label never moves, and a count says the number so nobody has to read a fill.

Two meanings

Modes

Band interactivePressed over interactiveDefault (primary) or bgBrandSubtle over bgPrimary (secondary, L only — textBrand on the band measures 3.6:1, large text only). The shipped green Accept Job becomes brand: there is no success Button tone. The last state is never a full-dark button — a countdown ends as disabled with its reason; progress ends as the outcome.

Tap it and it becomes progress — Checking in · 1 of 3, location → photo → server — not tappable, no spinner, and the outcome replaces it. Let the window lapse instead and the Button disables with the reason under it.

Same anatomy, opposite author

ProgressButton vs the hold Button

Both are a Button with a band. The difference is who moves it. Hold: her finger, danger tone, cancels on release (button.md § hold). ProgressButton: the clock or the server, brand tone, moves whether or not she touches the screen. Because they are two components, a moving fill never means two things — and a ProgressButton is never used on a danger action, where a self-moving band would look like the hold completing by itself.

One clock per screen

Rules

  1. Not beside a Timer of the same window. The job alert keeps its Timer pill and a plain Button; countdown lives on job rows and the Home card, where there is no pill.
  2. Band from the left, covering what is used or done — the direction of the hold sweep, the ProgressBar and the Stepper.
  3. Label still. Unchanged through a countdown; changed once, to the participle, at the start of progress.
  4. Count says the number in tabular numerals: 0:42, 2 of 3. The band alone is never the only channel.
  5. Ends as a normal Button. Countdown at 0 → disabled with the reason under it; progress at 100 → the outcome replaces it, or the Button returns with the failure under it.
  6. Brand only, L or M, one per screen. Never danger, never S, never icon-only.

What changes in Figma

  1. Progress Buttons (1491:13660) → ProgressButton: Mode (Countdown / Progress), Variant (Primary / Secondary), Size (L / M), Fill (0 / 25 / 50 / 75 / 100), Count (boolean). Band = left-anchored rectangle clipped by the container, bound to interactivePressed / bgBrandSubtle.
  2. Recolour the green Accept Job set to interactiveDefault; label stays textInverse.
  3. Replace the full-dark variant with the two end states: Countdown 100 = Button disabled + reason; Progress 100 = the outcome Tag.
  4. Button reconciliation item 6 (Progress Buttons out of the Components page) is superseded — they stay, as this component.

Result: 2 × 2 × 2 × 5 = 40 variants (Figma) replacing 10 loose ones. Contrast measured: textInverse on interactiveDefault 4.0:1, on interactivePressed 5.7:1; textBrand on bgBrandSubtle 3.6:1 (secondary is L only). The ButtonLg 18/24 decision applies here as it does to Button.
