## Boundary

This project reads **its own folder** and **the system** (`tokens/`, `components/`, `foundations/`, `principles/`, `patterns/`, `references/SCREENS.md`). It does not read any other project's folder. Every decision traces to this PRD, the principles, or the tokens — nothing else.

| **Paper does not inherit `fontFamily`.** Setting Outfit on an artboard (at creation or via `update_styles`) leaves every text node inside it on `system-ui`. | Font must be set **on each text node**, not on the frame. ~460 nodes were wrong on first build and were corrected on 2026-09-12. The 37 status-bar clocks stay `system-ui` deliberately — a real iOS status bar renders in the OS face, not the brand face. **Check with `find_nodes({filters:[{styleName:"font-family", styleValue:"system-ui, sans-serif"}]})` before calling any Paper board finished.** |
| **A board is a document. A screen is not finished until someone with no context can read it.** | Every screen carries a **caption**: its kind (`FULL SCREEN` · `BOTTOM SHEET over X` · `BRANCH of X · condition` · `STATE of X` · `DETAIL of X · optional`), **what opens it**, and why it exists. Every group of screens carries a **phase title** with its step range and a sentence on what the phase is for. Flows over ~6 steps **stack into phases** rather than running in one line — a 17-wide row has no reading order. |
| **A screen added mid-session is not done until it is placed, lettered and arrowed.** | Three separate turns added a variant without one, and the board had to be cleaned three times. On every new artboard: put it in its **parent's column**, in the **next free row** (2120 / 3060 / 3960), give it the **next letter in top-to-bottom order** — a flow branch outranks a state variant for the letter `a` — add the **down-arrow** at `col + 168`, and **update the cover counts**. |
| **A Paper artboard is a flex column; `top` on a `position: relative` child is an offset, not a coordinate.** | The rendered y is *flow position + `top`*. So setting `top: 732px` on a tab bar that `margin-top: auto` has already pushed to 732 renders it at **1464**, off the artboard. Children that are `position: absolute` (most cards) behave normally. **Before repositioning any child of an artboard, read `get_computed_styles` for its `position`** — if it is `relative`, set `top: 0` and let flow place it, or compute the offset from the preceding in-flow sibling. Hit on 2026-09-12 on 1.10b, where the tab bar was clipped off the bottom and the why-note floated 165 px low. |
| **Section labels must be sized for the board, not the screen.** | 22 px labels on a 3,700 × 7,400 px board are invisible at section zoom. Board-level labels are 96 px / 40 px. |
| **The daily inventory list must not be a 40–50 item list.** "First principally, we can't ask them to give 48 का list." | v1.1 | Effort scales with what changed today, not with total inventory. This is the load-bearing constraint of § 5. |

## Answered — from the v1.1 clarification call

Kept here rather than deleted, because the answer and the reason for it are both design inputs.

| # | Question | Answer (v1.1) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Does the expert pay for her replenishment? | **Yes.** "You are paying for each product. We are auto placing order for you, but end of the month, in the payout, I will deduct it." Pricing must be shown "because we are going to deduct that money." The replenishment screen is a **bill**, not a delivery tracker. It also answers the leakage concern — she has no incentive to resell stock she paid for. |
| 3 | Is a restock tick a checkbox or a quantity? | **Neither, as framed.** The real model: her **full weekly inventory lives at her home**; her bag is a subset. At logout the app shows only *what she used today* ("if they have used 10 items… show them that these 10 you used today, add them") and she physically refills her bag from home stock and confirms. So the daily list is the **consumption list**, generated by the system, not a stock count she compiles. |
| 4 | Time budget for the inventory update? | Not a number, but a rule: **the list is what changed today, never the full inventory.** "There is a 40-50 items का list… nobody looks at it." 4–10 items on a normal day. |
| 5 | What proves a spot check? | Nothing yet, and that is acknowledged. Spot checks are an **experiment**: "This is something जो open है, but it could work or it could not work. Learning है। हटा देंगे." Real enforcement is reactive — experts call support mid-job saying stock is missing. "I am first threatening them, then I will think of a solution of exact logic." → Design it as removable. |
| 6 | Weekoff | **One day a week, mandatory, unpaid, Monday–Thursday only** (never Sat/Sun). The day is **chosen once at go-live and then fixed** — "every Wednesday, for example." Not counted in bonuses "or anywhere." Rationale: seven continuous days affects presentation, and Beauty is a premium service. Househelp gets no leave at all. |
| 8 | Does the new-job alert still exist for Beauty? | **Yes** — same screen, different line items: services booked and **time per service** (calculated backwards from a system max, e.g. 60 min). Overrun is allowed, not blocked. |
| 19 | Does the report-issue flow exist? | **No — and there are two of them.** (a) At daily refill: "I said add 8 kits, assuming she has 8 kits at home. For some reason she doesn't. She has to report that." Put a button now, design the flow. (b) At delivery: wrong/missing/damaged item on the weekly order. |
| 23 | Is hiding the per-job amount deliberate? | **Yes, and the reason is reallocation churn.** A job allotted to her yesterday can be reallocated today — "you can be pissed off that my ₹3,000 job has gone to someone else." Services are shown first; if there is backlash, services get hidden too. Note this is a **stated intention to reduce transparency further**, which will need a principles conversation if it happens (P5). |
| — | Why show scheduled jobs at all? | Beauty experts have **vehicles and a hub** (not a hotspot). Hub round-trip is 10–15 min each way; a booking landing 25 min later makes the trip wasted. Seeing the schedule lets her decide whether to go back. |
| — | What is the boundary, really? | A **micro-market**, ~3.5 km, inside which she can log in **from anywhere** — the hotspot pin is gone. Outside it she **cannot log in**. Purpose is to communicate the serviceable area. |
| — | What triggers the weekly order? | Every 7th day, **two days before her weekoff**: system compares weekly should-have against live inventory (driven by daily consumption) and orders the difference. Placed automatically by tech, or manually by the business team. |
